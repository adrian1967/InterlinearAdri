It is recorded here to document its presence in the space separating the two columns, but it is not incorporated into the transcription of John.

### Right of the Column

First line: ΟΥΔΕΕΚΘΕΛΗΜΑ  
Second line: ΤΟCΑΝΔΡΟC

## Division Marks

**Line 12:** In the left margin, the Greek letter Β (beta), surmounted by a short horizontal stroke, is visible.

It corresponds to the number 2 in the ancient division system of Codex Vaticanus.

A short horizontal mark (*paragraphos*) is also visible beneath the beginning of the line.

A space separates ΟΥΚΑΤΕΛΑΒΕΝ from ΕΓΕΝΕ, at the beginning of John 1:6.

**Line 26:** A short horizontal mark is visible beneath the first letters of the line. A space also appears to separate two textual units.

This space occurs between CΜΟΝ, completing ΚΟCΜΟΝ from line 25, and ΕΝ, introducing ΕΝ Τω ΚΟCΜω ΗΝ.

This division corresponds to the transition between John 1:9 and John 1:10 in traditional verse numbering.

The precise chronology of the interventions that produced these marks, and their possible relationship to the Greek marginal numbers, remain to be investigated.

## Critical Notes

### Upper Title

The letters Α and Η in ΙωΑΝΗΝ are graphically uncertain in the photograph.

We provisionally retain this reading on the basis of the identifiable letters, the context, and the ancient spelling ΙωΑΝΗΝ, also attested in Papyrus P75.

No equally convincing alternative linguistic interpretation has been identified.

The two crosses visible on either side of the title are preserved in the diplomatic record.

### Pagination

The number 1349 is visible on the manuscript.

It may belong to a pagination system added after the original copying of the manuscript.

The date of this addition has not been established.

### Line 1

The first letter, transcribed as Ε, has an unusual graphic form and is not immediately recognizable as an epsilon.

Its strokes appear irregular, with a particularly prominent extension and middle bar.

We retain Ε because of the Greek context ΕΝΑΡΧΗ ("In the beginning"), while acknowledging that its graphic identification is not entirely certain.

The origin of these irregularities remains undetermined.

### Line 10

In CΚΟΤΙΑ, the letter transcribed as Τ has a form that may resemble Γ.

We retain Τ because of the attested Greek form CΚΟΤΙΑ and the context.

### Line 12

A trace resembling Α appears after ΟΥΚΑΤΕΛΑΒΕΝ, in a space where no letter would normally be expected on this side of the manuscript.

Examination of the reverse side of the parchment revealed an Α belonging to ΠΛΗΡωΜΑΤΟC (John 1:16), at a potentially corresponding position.

A yellowish stain is also visible in this region of the parchment.

It may be associated with a local change in transparency, making writing on the reverse side more visible.

The nature and origin of the stain remain undetermined.

We consider it highly probable that the observed trace comes from writing visible through the parchment rather than from an additional letter belonging to the main text.

A precise superimposition of photographs of both sides would strengthen this identification.

**Reading retained:** ΟΥΚΑΤΕΛΑΒΕΝ  ΕΓΕΝΕ.

The space between the two sequences is preserved because it also corresponds to a textual division mark.

### Line 15

We retain a single Ν in ΙωΑΝΗC, according to our reading of the photograph.

### Line 19

An apostrophe is visible after ΔΙ.

It most probably represents the elision of the final Α of ΔΙΑ before a vowel.

We preserve the observed form ΔΙ’.

### Line 21

An apostrophe is visible after ΑΛΛ.

It represents the elision of the final vowel of ΑΛΛΑ before ΙΝΑ.

We preserve ΑΛΛ’ in the transcription.

### Line 23

A lighter ink trace appears within ΑΛΗΘΙΝΟΝ.

Its origin remains undetermined.

Examination of the reverse side did not reveal a corresponding letter.

This trace is not treated as an additional letter.

### Line 31

A supralinear stroke appears to be present over the final Ο of ΠΑΡΕΛΑΒΟ̅.

It is provisionally preserved in the diplomatic transcription.

It may indicate an abbreviation of the final Ν, yielding ΠΑΡΕΛΑΒΟΝ in the expanded text.

Its presence and precise function still require confirmation from the original photograph.

### Line 32

The third Ο of the line, belonging to ΕΛΑΒΟΝ, has an unusual appearance that may resemble a Λ surmounted by a small ο.

Because the sign is graphically uncertain, we provisionally retain Ο, consistent with the Greek form ΕΛΑΒΟΝ.

This reading remains open to revision.

### Line 39

A small Ν appears above the line, between the ordinary Ν and the Η in ΕΓΕΝ⁽Ν⁾ΗΘΗCΑ̅.

We preserve this addition as ⁽Ν⁾.

A supralinear stroke has also been observed above the final Α.

It is represented as Α̅ and provisionally interpreted as an abbreviation of the final Ν.

The diplomatic transcription preserves the abbreviation mark without introducing an ordinary final Ν.

In the reading transcription, the word may be expanded as ΕΓΕΝΝΗΘΗCΑΝ.

### Lines 40–42

Some of the final letters are paler.

The final Ε of line 40 appears identifiable.

The readings Η at the end of line 41 and Α at the end of line 42 are provisionally retained despite their reduced visibility.

## Notes on Marginal Annotations

### Right of Line 9 — ΤωΝΑΝΘΡωΠωΝ

The marginal annotation ΤωΝΑΝΘΡωΠωΝ ("of human beings", "of mankind") was probably intended to complete the passage in John 1:4.

Its position in the margin does not, by itself, establish its exact point of insertion.

Two grammatical attachments have been considered:

- To ΖωΗ: "the life of mankind".
- To ΦωC: "the light of mankind".

Attachment to ΦωC corresponds to the traditional reading of the Greek passage.

However, in our study, the precise intention of the corrector remains an open question.

The annotation is therefore not provisionally incorporated into our reading translation.

It will be reconsidered in the light of context and grammatical analysis.

### Right of Lines 38–39

**First line**

Several letters are partially erased or difficult to identify.

Our preliminary graphic observation gives:

?ΥΔΕΕΚ?ΕΛ??Α

We propose the reconstruction ΟΥΔΕΕΚΘΕΛΗΜΑ on the basis of the identifiable letters and the context of John 1:13.

The four reconstructed letters (Ο, Θ, Η and Μ) are not all directly confirmed by the photograph.

This reading remains provisional.

**Second line**

We retain ΤΟCΑΝΔΡΟC.

The penultimate letter, Ο, is graphically uncertain.

This reading is retained because of the surrounding letters and its consistency with the first line.

Together, the two lines probably represent:

ΟΥΔΕ ΕΚ ΘΕΛΗΜΑΤΟC ΑΝΔΡΟC

"Nor of the will of a man."

The marginal position, beside the transition between ΘΕΛΗΜΑΤΟC CΑΡΚΟC and ΑΛΛ ΕΚ Θ̅Υ̅, supports the hypothesis that the corrector intended the expression to be inserted between these two clauses.

This insertion is retained in our reading transcription, with square brackets indicating its marginal origin.

### Left of Lines 39–40 — ΜΕΓΑΛΗC

A small inscription is identified as ΜΕΓΑΛΗC ("great").

It is most probably associated with Column II, containing the end of the Gospel of Luke, rather than Column III.

It probably completes ΧΑΡΑC ΜΕΓΑΛΗC ("great joy") in Luke 24:52.

Its position outside the main text indicates a marginal addition.

The date and author of this addition remain undetermined.
